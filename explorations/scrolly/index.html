01 · lobkit

A resting order.

lobkit rebuilds Kalshi limit-order books from L2 data. Start with one order: size 10, joining the back of an existing price level. Everyone already in line trades first.

The book on the stage is illustrative. The setup matches lobkit's test: size 10, back of the level, 60 second horizon.

02 · lobkit

Will it fill?

Trades eat the queue from the front. The question is whether enough volume arrives within 60 seconds to reach my order. lobkit answers with a probability, not a yes or no.

Illustrative drain. The readout steps through the model's real bin centers, from 0.014 to 0.449.

03 · real data, 2026-09-02

Predicted vs. observed.

A probability is only worth something if it is calibrated. lobkit placed 9,995 hypothetical orders across 209 markets and checked every prediction against what happened. A perfect model sits on the dashed line.

Brier
0.0502 vs 0.0539 base rate, skill +0.069
AUC
0.67 to 0.70 on a held-out half
Worst gap
0.078 in bins with n ≥ 25

04 · leakage control

The control.

Then I tried to break it. Pair each prediction with another level's outcome. If the pipeline leaked future data, the skill would survive the shuffle. It does not.

Skill
+0.069 real, −0.122 shuffled
Brier
0.0502 real, 0.0604 shuffled
0.6+ bins
observed 0.695 real, 0.055 shuffled

Prediction reads the tape before the cursor and resolution reads after it, so leakage is impossible by construction.

05 · stated limit

The limit.

44.6% of orders are ambiguous from L2 data alone. The level emptied, but trades alone did not clear the queue, so whether they filled depends on cancellations nobody can see. The base rate is 5.71% under the lower truth and 50.3% under the upper.

lobkit is calibrated against the lower, conservative truth. It is trusted for book reconstruction and cost accounting, and not trusted for point fill estimates. It says so itself.
